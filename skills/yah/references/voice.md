# Voice from a sample

Read this when the user gives a writing sample, pasted or in a file they name.

## Read the sample first

Before drafting, note how the writer actually writes:

- **Sentence length:** mostly short, mostly long, or mixed, and where they switch.
- **Word choice:** plain or formal, slang, swearing, recurring words and phrases, jargon they use without explaining.
- **Punctuation:** dashes, semicolons, parentheses, exclamation marks, ellipses, emoji, and how often.
- **Openings:** how sentences and paragraphs start. Do they open with "So", "Look", a question, the subject?
- **Contractions and person:** "I", "we", "you", or none; contractions or not.
- **Stance:** how sure they sound, how they show doubt, whether they joke.

## Write in that voice

Match those traits at about the same rate. In a short output, use each strong habit at least once if the writer would. The sample beats YAH's default style rules and the tell catalog wherever they conflict: if the writer uses dashes, keep dashes; if they open with "Look,", end on a blunt fragment, or write in lowercase, that's their voice, not a tell. The tell pass still removes patterns the sample doesn't show.

The sample never overrides the fact rules. Their voice, your facts: only what the user or the source supplied.

Don't copy the sample's sentences or its content into the new text. Match how they write, not what they wrote. Reusing a signature phrase or opener once ("Short version:") is matching; reusing their sentences is copying.

## Keep the sample private

Use the sample only in this conversation. Never write it, quote it, or save a summary of it to any file, memory, or profile. If the user wants a reusable voice, they keep the sample themselves.
