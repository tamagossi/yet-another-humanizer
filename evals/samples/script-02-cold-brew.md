# script-02-cold-brew

## Prompt

```txt
Write a 3-minute video script: how to make cold brew coffee at home. Use these: coarse grind, 1 part coffee to 8 parts water, steep 16 hours in the fridge, strain through a paper filter, dilute 1:1 with water or milk when serving.
```

## What to check

Uses only the supplied numbers; speakable; notes separate.

## Default output

### How to Make Cold Brew Coffee at Home (3-Minute Video Script)

**Estimated runtime:** ~3 minutes
**Tone:** Friendly, relaxed, easy to follow

---

**[0:00 – 0:15] INTRO / HOOK**

*[Visual: Slow-motion shot of cold brew being poured over ice in a tall glass. Ice cubes clink.]*

**HOST:**
Smooth, rich, and way less bitter than regular iced coffee. Cold brew is one of the easiest things you can make at home, and once you try it, you might never go back to buying it at the café. Let's make some!

*[Title card: "How to Make Cold Brew at Home"]*

---

**[0:15 – 0:40] WHAT YOU'LL NEED**

*[Visual: Overhead shot of ingredients and tools laid out on the counter.]*

**HOST:**
Here's everything you need:
- Coffee beans, whatever kind you like
- A grinder, or pre-ground coarse coffee
- Cold, filtered water
- A large jar or pitcher
- A paper coffee filter and a strainer or funnel
- And a little patience

*[On-screen text list appears as each item is mentioned.]*

That's it. No fancy equipment required.

---

**[0:40 – 1:10] STEP 1: GRIND COARSE**

*[Visual: Close-up of beans going into a grinder, then a shot of the coarse grounds in a hand.]*

**HOST:**
Step one: grind your coffee **coarse**. You want it to look like rough sea salt or breadcrumbs, not fine powder.

*[On-screen text: "Step 1: Coarse grind"]*

Why does this matter? A fine grind over-extracts during a long steep, which makes your coffee bitter and muddy. And it'll clog your filter later. Coarse grounds give you that clean, smooth flavor cold brew is known for.

---

**[1:10 – 1:40] STEP 2: MEASURE THE RATIO**

*[Visual: Coffee being measured on a kitchen scale, then poured into the jar. Water poured in after.]*

**HOST:**
Step two: get your ratio right. We're using **1 part coffee to 8 parts water**.

*[On-screen text: "Step 2: 1 part coffee : 8 parts water"]*

So, for example, if you use 100 grams of coffee, add 800 grams, or about 800 milliliters, of cold water. If you're measuring by volume, 1 cup of grounds to 8 cups of water works too.

Pour the water over the grounds and give it a gentle stir to make sure everything is fully soaked. No dry clumps!

*[Visual: Spoon stirring the mixture.]*

---

**[1:40 – 2:00] STEP 3: STEEP IN THE FRIDGE**

*[Visual: Lid going on the jar, then the jar being placed in the fridge. Quick time-lapse or clock graphic spinning.]*

**HOST:**
Step three: cover it up and let it steep in the fridge for **16 hours**.

*[On-screen text: "Step 3: Steep 16 hours in the fridge"]*

The easiest way to do this? Set it up in the evening, and it'll be ready by the next afternoon. The cold, slow extraction is what gives cold brew its sweet, mellow taste.

---

**[2:00 – 2:25] STEP 4: STRAIN THROUGH A PAPER FILTER**

*[Visual: Paper filter set in a funnel or strainer over a clean jar. Cold brew slowly poured through.]*

**HOST:**
Step four: strain it. Pour the mixture through a **paper filter** into a clean jar or bottle.

*[On-screen text: "Step 4: Strain through a paper filter"]*

The paper filter catches all the fine sediment, so your cold brew comes out clean and silky. It might take a few minutes, so just let gravity do the work. Don't squeeze the filter, or you'll push bitterness and grit into your coffee.

What you've got now is cold brew **concentrate**. It's strong, so don't drink it straight.

---

**[2:25 – 2:45] STEP 5: DILUTE AND SERVE**

*[Visual: Glass filled with ice. Concentrate poured halfway, then topped with milk swirling in.]*

**HOST:**
Step five: when you're ready to serve, dilute it **1 to 1**. Half concentrate, half water or milk.

*[On-screen text: "Step 5: Dilute 1:1 with water or milk"]*

Pour it over ice, and enjoy. Want it stronger? Add a little less water. Want it creamier? Go with milk or oat milk. Make it your own.

---

**[2:45 – 3:00] OUTRO**

*[Visual: Host taking a sip, smiling. Then a final beauty shot of the glass.]*

**HOST:**
And that's it! Coarse grind, 1 to 8 ratio, 16 hours in the fridge, strain, and dilute. Super simple, and so good.

If you try this recipe, let me know how it turns out in the comments. And don't forget to like and subscribe for more easy coffee recipes. See you next time!

*[End screen: Subscribe button and suggested videos.]*

## YAH output

[VISUAL: a glass of cold brew next to a mug of hot coffee being poured over ice]
Lots of people think cold brew is just regular coffee poured over ice. It's made a different way, and you can do it at home with a jar and your fridge.

[VISUAL: whole beans next to a pile of coarse grounds]
Start with the grind. You want it coarse, about like coarse sea salt. The coffee is going to sit in water for a long time, and fine grounds give up too much over those hours. The coffee comes out bitter and muddy. Fine grounds also clog the paper filter later, so straining drags on.

Pre-ground coffee is usually ground for drip machines, which is finer than you want here. Ask the shop to grind it coarse, or use the coarsest setting on your own grinder.

[ON-SCREEN TEXT: 1 part coffee : 8 parts water]
Next, the ratio. Use one part coffee to eight parts water. Put the grounds in a jar, pour in the water, and stir until every bit of coffee is wet. Dry clumps floating on top won't brew evenly.

[VISUAL: lid going on the jar, jar going into the fridge]
Put a lid on the jar and leave it in the fridge for sixteen hours. This step is where cold brew gets its name. Hot water pulls the flavor out of coffee in a few minutes. Cold water does the same job slowly, over hours. That slow extraction is why cold brew tends to taste smoother and less bitter than hot coffee that's been chilled.

If you start it at six in the evening, it's ready at ten the next morning.

[VISUAL: paper filter in a pour-over cone over a jug, coffee dripping through]
When the sixteen hours are up, strain it through a paper filter. A coffee filter in a pour-over cone works, or one set inside a sieve over a bowl. Pour slowly and let it drip through. The paper catches the fine silt that a metal strainer lets through, so there's no grit at the bottom of your glass.

[VISUAL: strained coffee in a bottle]
What you have now is a concentrate. At one part coffee to eight parts water, it's too strong to drink straight.

[VISUAL: concentrate poured into a glass of ice, then topped with milk]
To serve it, dilute it one to one. Pour some concentrate into a glass, add the same amount of water or milk, and drink it over ice if you like.
